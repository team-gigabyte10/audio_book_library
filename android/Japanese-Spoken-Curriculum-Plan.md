# Japanese Spoken Course — Standard Curriculum Plan (Zero → N5 → N1 → Advanced)

**Learner:** Bangla speaker. Bangla is the language of instruction and meaning.
**Goal:** Speak Japanese, from zero knowledge to a standard, professional level.
**Scope:** The Japanese course only. This plan does not depend on the current app structure. Section 9 lists the rules that keep the English spoken course unchanged.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Speaking first.** Every lesson ends with the learner speaking aloud. Reading and writing support the speech; they are not the goal.
2. **One standard ladder.** Levels follow the JLPT order (N5 → N1). The JLPT tests reading and listening only, so the speaking goals use the **JF Standard / CEFR "can-do"** statements (A1 → C2).
3. **Bangla word order is an advantage.** Bangla and Japanese are both SOV (subject-object-verb). Show the word-by-word match early:
   আমি ভাত খাই। = 私は ご飯を 食べます。 (watashi wa gohan o tabemasu)
4. **Polite form first, plain form later.** Start with です/ます. Teach plain (casual) speech at N4, and full keigo (honorific language) from N3.
5. **Script grows slowly.** Romaji and a Bangla pronunciation hint (`bn_pron`) are supports at the start. Kana replaces romaji during Stage 0. Kanji come gradually from N5, for reading only, with furigana (section 11.1–11.2).
6. **Small steps.** Each lesson has 5–10 new items and takes 15–20 minutes.
7. **Native review is required.** A native Japanese speaker checks every item before release. Machine drafts are never shown to learners unreviewed.

## 3. Curriculum sequence

Each **unit** = 4–5 lessons + 1 review lesson. Each level ends with a **speaking test** (section 6).

### Stage 0 — Zero start (Pre-A1): letters, words and sound fluency

**Goal of Stage 0:** the learner says every Japanese sound correctly and quickly before grammar starts. Every letter comes with real words, and every word is practised aloud.

**Stage 0 has 10 units and about 50 lessons (40–60 guided hours).**

| Unit | Content | Lessons | Speaking task at the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | Vowels and mora rhythm | 4 | Say あいうえお and clap the beats of 10 words |
| 0.2 | Hiragana あ・か・さ・た・な rows | 6 | Read 25 words aloud |
| 0.3 | Hiragana は・ま・や・ら・わ rows + ん | 6 | Read 25 words aloud |
| 0.4 | Voiced sounds: が ざ だ ば ぱ | 5 | Say 10 voiced / unvoiced pairs |
| 0.5 | Combined sounds: きゃ しゅ ちょ … | 5 | Read 15 combined-sound words |
| 0.6 | Special sounds: long vowels, small っ, ん | 6 | Say 10 minimal pairs without a mistake |
| 0.7 | Katakana and loanwords | 6 | Say your name and city in katakana |
| 0.8 | Pitch accent and intonation | 4 | Say 3 pitch pairs and 2 question tones |
| 0.9 | Survival phrases and numbers | 5 | 1-minute greeting role-play |
| 0.10 | Fluency drills and Stage 0 test | 3 | Stage 0 speaking test |

#### How every letter lesson works (sound practice loop)

1. **Hear** the letter 3 times (slow → normal → slow).
2. **See** the mouth tip in Bangla (for example "ঠোঁট গোল করবেন না").
3. **Say** the letter after the model, 3 times.
4. **Word drill:** hear and repeat 5 words that use the letter.
5. **Contrast drill:** hear a near pair (for example つ / す) and choose the one you hear.
6. **Speed round:** say the 5 words at 0.8x → 1.0x → 1.2x.
7. **Recall:** see the Bangla meaning, then say the Japanese word.

#### Pronunciation notes for Bangla speakers

- **た・て・と and だ・で・ど are dental, like Bangla ত and দ**, not ট and ড. Bangla speakers have an advantage here.
- **う is not rounded.** Say উ with flat, relaxed lips.
- **さ・す・せ・そ use English "s"**, not Bangla "শ". Only し is "শি".
- **ざ・ず・ぜ・ぞ use English "z".** Bangla has no z, so practise "য" with a buzz.
- **ら行 is one quick tap** of the tongue, between Bangla র and ল. Do not roll it.
- **ふ is soft:** blow air through nearly closed lips. Do not touch the teeth to the lip.
- **つ = "ৎসু"**: t and s together in one sound.
- **Each mora (beat) has the same length.** がっこう = が・っ・こ・う = 4 beats.

#### Unit 0.7 — Katakana and loanwords

- **Full katakana set**, then the long-vowel mark ー and small ッ.
- **Everyday loanwords:** コーヒー (কফি), パン (রুটি), テレビ (টিভি), ノート (খাতা), バス (বাস), タクシー (ট্যাক্সি), スマホ (স্মার্টফোন), アイスクリーム (আইসক্রিম), コンピューター (কম্পিউটার)
- **New sounds for loanwords:** ファ (ファイル, file), ティ (パーティー, party), ディ (ディズニー, Disney)
- **Your own names:** バングラデシュ (Bangladesh), ダッカ (Dhaka), チッタゴン (Chittagong), ラヒム (Rahim), ファティマ (Fatima)
- **Drill:** hear an English word, then say it the Japanese way (computer → コンピューター).

#### Unit 0.10 — Fluency drills

- **Shadowing:** a 6-line greeting dialogue. Speak along with the audio at 0.8x, then at 1.0x.
- **Speed ladder:** say 10 phrases at 0.8x → 1.0x → 1.2x.
- **Beginner tongue twisters (早口言葉):**
  1. なまむぎ なまごめ なまたまご (raw wheat, raw rice, raw egg)
  2. すもも も もも も もも の うち (plums and peaches are both kinds of peach)
  3. となりの きゃくは よく かき くう きゃくだ (the guest next door eats a lot of persimmons)
  4. あかまきがみ あおまきがみ きまきがみ (red, blue and yellow scroll paper)
- **Stage 0 speaking test:**
  1. Read 20 random kana aloud.
  2. Say 10 minimal pairs correctly.
  3. Give a 30-second self-introduction.
  4. Do a 1-minute greeting role-play.

## 4. Lesson template (same for every level)

1. **Warm-up (2 min):** review 3 old items from the review queue.
2. **Listen (3 min):** model dialogue at slow speed, then normal speed. Shadow it (speak along).
3. **New items (5 min):** for each item, Japanese → Bangla meaning → Japanese, with word-by-word gloss and particle notes.
4. **Pattern drill (4 min):** the **6-way transformation** of the lesson sentence:
   1. polite affirmative (食べます)
   2. polite negative (食べません)
   3. polite past (食べました)
   4. polite past negative (食べませんでした)
   5. plain form (食べる / 食べない), from N4
   6. question (食べますか)
5. **Speak (4 min):** see the Bangla, say the Japanese aloud, then hear the model answer.
6. **Role-play (from N5 unit 5.10):** two-person dialogue; the learner takes one role.
7. **Quiz (2 min):** listen → choose the meaning; Bangla → choose the Japanese; arrange the words; choose the particle.
8. **Review queue:** spaced repetition schedules the new items.

### Pronunciation points for Bangla speakers

| Japanese sound | Common problem | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| つ (tsu) | Said as "চু" or "সু" | ৎসু hint, minimal pairs つ/す/ちゅ |
| ふ (fu) | Said as hard "ফু" | Soft lips, blow air |
| ら行 (r) | Rolled "র" | Single quick tap |
| Long vowels | Too short | おばさん / おばあさん, clap the mora |
| Small っ | Not held | きて / きって, pause one beat |
| Pitch accent | Flat or stress-based | 箸 (はし) / 橋 (はし), pitch graph |

## 8. Content production pipeline

1. **Write the syllabus:** units, grammar points and vocabulary lists per level (this document → per-unit sheets).
2. **Draft items:** a writer (or an AI draft, marked `draft`) writes sentences with Bangla meanings.
3. **Add readings:** furigana, romaji and pitch from tools, then check by hand.
4. **Bangla hints:** generate `bn_pron` from the reading with a rule table, then fix by hand.
5. **Native review (required):** a native Japanese speaker checks naturalness, politeness level and pitch.
6. **Bangla review:** a Bangla speaker checks that every meaning is natural.
7. **Audio check:** listen to every item at slow and normal speed on 2 phones.
8. **Release:** set `status: reviewed` and publish the level.

## 11. Decisions

These 6 points were open. Each one now has a decision and a reason.

### 11.1 Romaji and the Bangla pronunciation hint

**Decision:**
- Units 0.1–0.3: show romaji and the Bangla hint (`bn_pron`) with every kana.
- From unit 0.4 to the end of N5: hide romaji by default (tap to show). Keep the Bangla hint on.
- N4: hide both by default (tap to show).
- From N3: no romaji and no Bangla hint. Learners read kana and kanji only.

**Reason:** romaji makes learners use English sounds (for example "tsu" read as "চু"). Bangla script is closer to Japanese sounds than English spelling is, so the Bangla hint stays longer than romaji. The tap-to-show option lets a learner check a word without depending on it.

### 11.2 Kanji: read only, or also write?

**Decision:** the spoken course teaches kanji **for reading only**. Writing kanji is an optional extra module.
- N5–N4: furigana on every kanji.
- N3–N2: furigana only on kanji from a higher level or new in the lesson.
- N1 and above: no furigana by default (tap to show the reading).

**Reason:** the goal is speaking. Writing practice takes a lot of time and does not improve speech. Reading kanji is still needed, because real life (signs, menus, work documents) uses kanji.

### 11.3 Pitch accent display

**Decision:**
- Stage 0 to N4: show a pitch line on every new word and in every pronunciation drill.
- N3 and above: show the pitch line on new words only, and on tap for other words.
- Use the Tokyo standard accent. Use a pitch-accent dictionary (for example the NHK accent dictionary or OJAD) as the source, and a native speaker checks it.

**Reason:** pitch accent is hard to fix later. Early, constant exposure builds the habit. At higher levels the line becomes noise, so it shows only when it helps.

### 11.4 Speech check in the "Speak" step

**Decision:** yes, from Stage 0 unit 0.2, but **only as feedback, never as a gate**.
- Use on-device `ja-JP` speech recognition and compare the recognised text with the target.
- Show a simple result: ✓ (match), "try again" (near match), or "listen and repeat" (no match).
- If speech recognition is not available on the phone, the learner rates themselves (😀 / 😐 / 😟).
- Progress depends only on quizzes and level tests, not on speech recognition.

**Reason:** speech recognition on budget phones is not reliable for accented speech. A wrong "fail" makes learners stop. Feedback still helps them speak more.

### 11.5 Native reviewers and review speed

**Decision:**
- At least **2 native Japanese reviewers**: one for naturalness and politeness level, one for pitch and audio.
- **1 Bangla reviewer** for the Bangla meanings and hints.
- Target: about 150 items per reviewer per week.
- Phase 1 (Stage 0 + N5) has about 900 items. With 2 reviewers this takes about 3–4 weeks of review.
- No item is released without the review status `reviewed`.

**Reason:** the review speed decides the roadmap speed. Two separate checks (language and sound) find different errors.

### 11.6 Which advanced tracks come first

**Decision (in this order):**
1. **Workplace Japanese for jobs in Japan:** caregiving (介護), food service and manufacturing. Many Bangladeshi learners go to Japan through the Specified Skilled Worker (特定技能) route.
2. **JFT-Basic exam preparation**, placed at the end of N4. The Japan Foundation Test for Basic Japanese (A2 level) is used for the Specified Skilled Worker visa.
3. **IT Japanese** for software and engineering jobs.
4. **Business Japanese (BJT level).**
5. **Interpreting Bangla ↔ Japanese.**
6. **Public speaking, native listening and culture.**

**Reason:** most learners study Japanese to work in Japan. Job-focused speaking and the JFT-Basic test give them the fastest real benefit. Check the current visa and test rules before you build each track, because these rules change.
